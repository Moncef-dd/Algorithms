# Public record barely registers Numidia Mind Technologies

Almost nothing about Numidia Mind Technologies (NMT) can be established from public sources: its founding date, founders, team, legal form, registration numbers, funding, revenue, clients and partners were all searched for and not found, and there is no basis for any valuation figure. The research ran on 16 November 2026 with web search only. **The company's own websites (numidiamind.com and numidiatec.com), LinkedIn, Hugging Face, the Wayback Machine, RDAP/WHOIS and nearly every news and vendor page cited below could not be opened from the research environment**, so most of this report rests on search-engine summaries of pages, not on the pages themselves. Only three company-specific facts were read first-hand, all on GitHub: an account named "Numidia Mind Technologies" with one empty repository, a 404 on a different guessed handle, and an OCR'd list that places the company among firms registered for a student employability fair on 15 November 2025. None of the five catalogue products (NMT Shield, NMT KYC 360, NMT SDR, TeamPilot, NMT 4n6) has any public trace tied to the company. The market side is much better documented: Algeria has about 30 regulated financial buyers, a KYC and anti-money-laundering rulebook rewritten in 2024–2026, data-transfer and import rules that favour local on-premise vendors, and no visible Algerian competitor in fraud scoring, AML monitoring or forensic software. The practical conclusion is that the company's profile has to be completed from primary sources (the commercial register, the company itself, a direct read of its sites), because the open web does not hold it.

Two inferences follow, and both remain unconfirmed. The two domains very probably belong to the same company (same name, same acronym, same office number in the same estate), but which is current, and whether a rebrand or a move within the estate explains the differences, cannot be settled without WHOIS and archive dates. The August 2026 date on the empty `nmt-website` repository suggests the web presence was built or reworked recently; that rests on a single date.

**Not found.** No individual can be tied to the company, so no name should be attributed to it. No source gives its legal form, capital or headcount, and no job posting by it was found; the only hiring signal is the fair, where participating companies collected applications for jobs, internships and final-year projects ([ensia-impact-chatbot, messages_enriched.json](https://github.com/61j3t/ensia-impact-chatbot/blob/main/data/messages_enriched.json)). **No funding round, investor, grant, revenue figure or valuation exists in any source found, and this report offers no estimate of one.** No client, pilot or reference was found. A separate list of ENSIA's partnerships in progress, dated 12 November 2025, does not include NMT. No tender, award, exhibition, hackathon, prize, article, interview or demo was found.

## Five product names leave no trace beyond the catalogue

NMT Shield, NMT KYC 360, NMT SDR, TeamPilot and NMT 4n6 are known only from the company's catalogue as supplied in the brief. Searches for each returned unrelated products. First-mention dates, maturity, model families, OCR engine, Arabic support, hosting model, certifications and measured results are all unknown from public sources, so any performance or pricing figure would have to come from the vendor. If the homepage does omit the product names, as the search summaries state, the catalogue circulates off-site, by PDF or direct sales.

The names carry confusion risks worth managing. The most serious is **TeamPilot**: an existing German product, "Teampilot AI" at teampilot.ai, sits in the same category (an AI workspace for companies and teams), is attributed to Techstack GmbH, founded 2023, and lists subscriptions at US$15–50 per month ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/teampilot-ai/about); [X @TeampilotAI](https://x.com/teampilotai)). Nothing suggests a link between the two, and no trademark-register search was run, so this is a similarity note and not a legal opinion. "NMT" itself collides with neural machine translation and a crypto token, "SDR" is widely read as software-defined radio, and the French company NuMind publishes extraction and OCR models under a near-identical name ([NuMind](https://numind.ai/about)).

The company name has its own crowd of namesakes, none linked to NMT. The highest-risk one is the Numidia Institute of Technology, a private AI-themed university in Algiers founded by Belgacem Haba and accredited in 2022, which dominates press results for "Numidia" plus "technology" ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/belgacem-haba-cree-le-nit-un-institut-des-technologies-en-algerie/)). Others include NumidiaTech (a separate cloud and hosting platform at numidiatech.com), Numidia Webs SARL, Numidia IT Business, numidia.io, Numida Technologies and NUMIDIA BV. Two IT companies registered in the same Kouba estate, City Lab and Aryc Tek, are neighbours only; no shared phone, email or person was found.

## A small, state-dominated buyer base under new compliance rules

Every figure in this and the following sections comes from search summaries of the cited pages; primary legal texts are linked where the search surfaced them but were not opened.

NMT's banking products address a concentrated market. After Ziraat Bankasi's licence in January 2025 the central bank's list counted **21 banks, 7 public and 14 foreign-capital**, plus 8 financial institutions a year earlier ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/la-banque-dalgerie-devoile-la-nouvelle-liste-des-banques-agreees/)). The seven public banks ran 1,272 of 1,673 branches in 2024 ([Algérie Eco](https://algerie-eco.com/?p=225939)). Algérie Poste is the single largest estate: of roughly 21.6 to 21.9 million interbank cards at end-2025, about four in five are its Edahabia cards ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/lalgerie-80-000-tpe-pour-plus-dun-million-de-commercants-a-fin-2025/)). Digital volumes are growing fast from a low base: **electronic payments reached DZD 939 billion in 2025, up 46% from DZD 643.8 billion**, and terminal payments doubled to DZD 89.5 billion ([Radio Algérie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/80108)). New entrants are coming through a fresh licensing stack: Règlement 25-02 for payment service providers ([Banque d'Algérie](https://www.bank-of-algeria.dz/stoodroa/2025/05/REG.N%C2%B025-02.FR_.pdf)), Règlement 24-04 for digital banks, and in September 2026 a first authorisation for Djezzy Payment Services to constitute itself as a provider ([Algérie Eco](https://algerie-eco.com/2026/09/23/banque-dalgerie-djezzy-payment-services-autorisee-a-se-constituer-en-psp/)). The upshot is a relationship-driven enterprise market of roughly 30 regulated institutions, not a volume market.

Regulation is the demand driver for fraud scoring and KYC. FATF grey-listed Algeria in October 2024 and removed it at the June 2026 plenary ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html)). In between, the country rewrote its AML law (Loi 25-10 of July 2025), the central bank replaced and then tightened its AML regulation (24-03, amended by 25-14) ([Banque d'Algérie](https://www.bank-of-algeria.dz/stoodroa/2025/10/REG-N%C2%B025-14-FR.pdf)), and **Instruction 04-2026 of 30 April 2026 set the first unified KYC procedure** for banks, financial institutions and Algérie Poste: risk-based identification, document consistency checks and mandatory consultation of the beneficial-ownership register ([Radio Algérie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/85371)). These obligations outlast the grey list, and the January 2026 update of the EU high-risk list still included Algeria, with no later delisting found ([Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/eu-list-of-highrisk-countries-and-jurisdictions-for-money-laundering-and-terrorism-financing-amended)). The "help exit the grey list" argument is therefore historical; sustaining effectiveness is the live one. Published fraud data is thin and consists of police case counts, not bank losses: the DGSN recorded **13,204 cybercrime cases involving 7,815 people in 2025** ([We Are Tech](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/tech/algerie-la-cybercriminalite-en-forte-hausse-plus-de-13-000-affaires-recensees-en-2025)). No official card-fraud statistics and no suspicious-transaction-report counts after 2018 were found.

The sovereignty pitch has real legal support. Loi 18-07 requires prior authorisation from the data-protection authority for any transfer of personal data abroad and prohibits transfers that could harm public security or the State's vital interests ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=DZ&t=law)); Loi 25-11 of July 2025 added a mandatory data protection officer, processing records and breach notification ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/protection-de-donnees-personnelles-nouvelle-obligation-en-algerie/)). Loi 26-02 of February 2026 requires trust-service providers to host data nationally and anchors electronic identification on the biometric ID card ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/transactions-et-signature-electroniques-lalgerie-franchit-un-grand-pas/)), and Décret présidentiel 26-07 obliges every public body to run a dedicated information-security unit ([APA News](https://fr.apanews.net/security/algerie-un-cadre-reglementaire-renforce-pour-la-cybersecurite-du-secteur-public/)). Since July 2025 every import of services needs prior ministerial authorisation before bank domiciliation ([L'Economiste Maghrébin](https://www.leconomistemaghrebin.com/2025/07/10/algerie-importations-services/)), and the list of services covered includes software acquisition and licence renewals ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/importations-de-services-lalgerie-fixe-la-liste-des-prestations-soumises-au-ppi/)). Two limits apply. No text was found that flatly bans foreign cloud or SaaS in ordinary banks, so a formal ban should not be asserted. And the same rules bind NMT: its products process identity and financial data, which brings declaration or authorisation duties of its own, and it would face the same import regime for GPUs and foreign licences.

National AI policy is active but light on procurement mechanics. A first strategy was presented in December 2024, a new draft went to the government on 25 May 2026 ([Radio Algérie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/86941)), and the State is developing a domestic AI model for tax legislation ([Afrik.com](https://www.afrik.com/algerie-pourquoi-l-etat-developpe-une-intelligence-artificielle-dediee-a-sa-legislation-fiscale)). No AI procurement framework or budget was found. For startups, the label regime brings a tax exemption of four years, renewable for two ([Maghreb Emergent](https://maghrebemergent.news/fr/start-up-et-incubateurs-en-algerie-jusqua-6-ans-dexoneration-fiscale-comment-en-beneficier/)), but the venture market offers few benchmarks: the largest local-currency round on record is VOLZ's DZD 600 million Series A in December 2025 ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2025/12/algeria-volz-raises-5-million-landmark-funding-round)), and no Algerian AI or fintech round with a disclosed valuation was found. Sources conflict on how many startups hold the label (more than 1,100 per the ministry, 2,300 per TSA). **No credible Algeria-specific market size exists for fraud detection, KYC, ID verification or digital forensics**; the published AI, cloud and cybersecurity estimates are low-reliability model outputs, and counting the regulated buyers is the only defensible sizing method.

## Incumbent modules and free software compete; local rivals are few

No Algerian startup in fraud scoring, AML monitoring or forensic software was found. Competition comes from elsewhere, and it differs by product.

|---|---|---|---|
| NMT Shield (fraud) | BPC SmartVista at SATIM and Algérie Poste; SironAML at Société Générale Algérie | Modules of installed core and switch platforms (Temenos, SBS, Finacle, SAB); source-available Marble; open-source Tazama | Vneuron (Tunisia), no Algerian client found |

| TeamPilot | No packaged self-hosted assistant found | Open WebUI, Dify, AnythingLLM, LibreChat, Onyx; Mistral Le Chat Enterprise self-hosted | AventureCloudz (Djezzy, Algeria Venture, Taubyte); Fentech's Hadretna; Nojoom.ai |
| NMT 4n6 | Paraben reseller Transmission-dz in Algiers | Foreign suites priced per examiner seat; open-source Autopsy | None found |

In banks, the documented incumbents are platforms, not best-of-breed fraud vendors. BPC states that SATIM and Algérie Poste both run SmartVista ([BPC](https://www.bpcbt.com/blog/bpc-facilitates-interoperability-in-algeria-millions-to-use-country-wide-network)), and a university thesis describes SironAML at Société Générale Algérie ([Univ. Béjaïa](https://www.univ-bejaia.dz/jspui/bitstream/123456789/4237/1/Blanchiment%20D%27argent.pdf)). For NICE Actimize, SAS, Oracle, FICO, Feedzai and the other global names, no sourced Algerian deployment was found; their presence is unverified, not disproved. The fraud engine actually running at SATIM or Algérie Poste is not named in any source. Vneuron, a Tunisian compliance vendor claiming more than 200 clients in 28 countries, is the most credible regional rival, though no Algerian client was found for it.

In identity verification, the closest like-for-like local competitor is **CORTIXIA**, a digital identity and KYC startup with a strategic partnership with state-backed Algeria Venture and no disclosed funding ([MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=44803)). Banxy's onboarding is the one documented bank deployment, reported as hosted entirely in Algeria ([Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/creation-de-compte-bancaire-la-nouvelle-methode-de-natixis/)). Tunisian Thinkneo says it is prospecting Algeria ([Managers.tn](https://managers.tn/2026/02/27/thinkneo-lidentite-numerique-tunisienne-face-a-la-fraude/)). A medium-term risk sits in the law itself: once chip-based electronic identification under Loi 26-02 is operational, registry-backed verification could displace image OCR for Algerian nationals.

For a self-hosted assistant, the real competitor is an integrator or in-house team installing a free open-source stack. Open WebUI and Dify each had roughly 160,000 GitHub stars when read on 16 November 2026, and AnythingLLM, LibreChat and Onyx ship agents, document retrieval and MCP under MIT licences ([Dify](https://github.com/langgenius/dify); [AnythingLLM](https://github.com/Mintplex-Labs/anything-llm)). A paid local platform has to win on packaging, language quality, support and compliance paperwork, since the licence cost of the alternative is zero. Algerian activity in this segment is infrastructure and models, such as the AventureCloudz development cloud hosted on Djezzy's infrastructure ([Developing Telecoms](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/enterprise-ecosystems/20197-djezzy-taubyte-and-algeria-venture-launch-ai-development-platform.html)).

Forensics is the segment with the least visible competition and the most opaque procurement. No public source ties any major forensic suite to an Algerian agency, the only local channel found is a Paraben reseller in Algiers ([Paraben](https://paraben.com/?p=1530)), and capability building runs through state-to-state agreements with the UK and South Korea ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202511/algeria-to-get-uk-support-on-digital-forensics-biometrics-for-policing)). Buyers are few (police, gendarmerie, justice) and no tender or price data was found. The notes infer that Israeli-origin Cellebrite is politically hard for Algerian state bodies to buy; that is an inference from the vendor's origin, not a documented procurement fact.

## Conclusion

The asymmetry is the finding. The market NMT says it serves is unusually well-suited to a local vendor on paper: a few dozen obliged buyers, compliance rules that are new and specific, legal friction against foreign SaaS, and no entrenched Algerian rival in three of five segments. Yet the company itself is nearly invisible, and the scarcest asset in this market, a referenceable bank or ministry deployment, is exactly what no public source shows for it. Whether that reflects a pre-commercial company or one that sells quietly cannot be determined from outside. The same structural protections also shelter foreign platforms already installed on-premise under long maintenance contracts, so the opening is narrower than the regulatory picture alone suggests.

Closing the gaps needs primary sources, not more searching. The commercial register (sidjilcom.cnrc.dz) would settle founding date, legal form, capital and managers; a direct read of both domains, their WHOIS records and archive captures would settle which site is current and what the company says about its products; startup.dz would settle label status; and the company itself is the only possible source for clients, revenue, team and any figure on which a valuation could rest. Until then, any number attached to this company, valuation included, would be invented.

## 1. Banking sector structure, payments volumes, switch projects, PSP / digital-bank / eKYC licensing

### Takeaway
A small, state-dominated banking system (about 21 banks, 7 of them public with ~3/4 of branches, plus Algérie Poste which issues ~80% of all cards) is going through a fast, regulator-driven digitisation: ~21.6-21.9 million cards and DZD 939 bn of e-payments in 2025 (+46%), a national mobile switch live since 2024, and a brand-new licensing stack for PSPs (Règlement 25-02) and digital banks (Règlement 24-04). The number of institutional buyers is small (roughly 30 regulated entities), but each is now obliged to deploy KYC, monitoring and fraud controls on rapidly growing digital volumes.

### Inferences
- Addressable regulated buyers for fraud scoring and KYC scoring are few: ~21 banks, ~8 financial institutions, Algérie Poste, GIE Monétique/SATIM, and a pipeline of new PSPs (Djezzy first) and possible digital banks. This is a concentrated, relationship-driven enterprise market, not a volume market; seven public banks plus Algérie Poste hold most accounts, branches and cards.
- Algérie Poste is the single largest card and account estate (about 80% of cards, 21 million+ active accounts) and therefore the largest single fraud-scoring and ID-verification opportunity; it is explicitly in scope of the Banque d'Algérie KYC instruction (see section 2).
- Algeria has roughly the same number of cards as Morocco but far lower usage: DZD 89.5 bn of POS payments is on the order of USD 0.7 bn versus Morocco's MAD 78 bn (roughly USD 8 bn). Usage is growing from a low base at 46-100%+ per year, so fraud exposure is likely to grow faster than fraud tooling.
- Digital-bank rules requiring a 30% Algerian-bank shareholder mean new digital entrants will be bank-anchored joint ventures; a vendor already inside an incumbent bank is positioned for the spin-off.
- Every new PSP and digital bank must build KYC/AML/fraud stacks from zero before the agrément, which creates greenfield demand for 2026-2028.

## 2. AML/CFT and fraud regulation; FATF, EU list; demand for transaction monitoring and KYC scoring

### Takeaway
The regulatory driver peaked in 2024-2026: Algeria was grey-listed by FATF in October 2024, rewrote its AML law (Loi 25-10, July 2025), tightened the Banque d'Algérie AML règlement (24-03, amended by 25-14) and issued a first unified KYC procedure (Instruction 04-2026, 30 April 2026), and was removed from the grey list on 19 June 2026 after an on-site visit. The compliance obligations created during the sprint are permanent, and Algeria still appears to be on the EU high-risk list, so banks must keep demonstrating effective risk-based KYC and monitoring; however, the "exit the grey list" urgency argument is now historical.

## 3. Data protection and sovereignty: Loi 18-07, ANPDP, localisation, cloud, cybersecurity bodies

### Takeaway
Algerian law makes local hosting the default path for sensitive workloads: cross-border transfers of personal data need prior ANPDP authorisation (Loi 18-07 art. 44, tightened by Loi 25-11 of July 2025), licensed cloud providers must locate infrastructure and customer data in Algeria (ARPCE cloud specifications), trust-service providers must host data nationally (Loi 26-02, Feb 2026), and since January 2026 every public body must have a dedicated information-security unit under the Defence Ministry-led national framework (Décret présidentiel 26-07). This is a real structural advantage for a self-hosted, locally supported vendor, but no retrieved text flatly bans foreign SaaS in banks; the restriction operates through authorisation requirements, FX friction and state policy.

### Cited Findings

**Loi 18-07 and ANPDP**
- Loi n° 18-07 of 10 June 2018 on protection of natural persons in personal-data processing (JO n° 34) — [Upgrowth JO copy](https://www.upgrowth.dz/journal-officiel/loi-18-07-protection-donnees-personnelles)
- Operators had one year from the ANPDP's installation to comply; that period ended on 11 August 2023, i.e. the law has been fully enforceable since August 2023 — [CMS Expert Guide](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/algeria2)
- Processing requires a prior declaration to, or authorisation from, the ANPDP; administrative sanctions include temporary (up to one year) or definitive withdrawal of the declaration receipt/authorisation plus fines; criminal penalties range from two months to five years' imprisonment, doubled for repeat offences; fines reported at DZD 20,000 to 1,000,000 — [CMS Expert Guide](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/algeria2); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=DZ&t=law)
- Art. 44: a controller may transfer personal data to a foreign state only with ANPDP authorisation and only if that state ensures an adequate level of protection; transfer is prohibited in all cases where it may harm public security or the vital interests of the State; art. 45 lists derogations — [Upgrowth JO copy](https://www.upgrowth.dz/journal-officiel/loi-18-07-protection-donnees-personnelles); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=DZ&t=law)

### Inferences
- For a bank or administration, putting customer or citizen data into a foreign SaaS/LLM API triggers an ANPDP transfer authorisation that is hard to obtain in practice, plus a "vital interests of the State" prohibition that is undefined and therefore risk-averse buyers will read broadly. Self-hosted, on-premise or locally hosted AI removes the question entirely. This is the strongest regulatory support for the vendor's sovereignty pitch.
- Décret 26-07 creates a named security function in every public body from 2026; those units are natural evaluators (and potential blockers) of any AI platform, and natural co-buyers of forensics/incident tooling.
- The State is concentrating public workloads in its own data centres and a planned national cloud. A sovereign AI platform sold to administrations will probably need to be deployable inside those facilities (HCN data centre / national cloud) rather than in the vendor's own hosting.
- The ANPDP regime also binds the vendor: its OCR, KYC and conversational products process identity and financial data, so declaration/authorisation, a DPO and processing records are prerequisites to sell.

- No Banque d'Algérie text specifically governing outsourcing, cloud use or data localisation by banks was found. Whether banks may use foreign cloud at all (and under what approval) is unverified; do not assert a formal ban.
- No published ANPDP enforcement statistics (formal notices, sanctions, number of declarations/authorisations) were found.
- No explicit government circular banning foreign messaging/cloud in administrations was found.
- Real capacity (MW, racks, GPU) of national data centres was not found.
- The .com.dz hosting requirement of Loi 18-05 concerns e-commerce operators; later amendments to Loi 18-05 were only seen via a low-reliability blog.

### Inferences
- The State's own choice to build a tax-law AI model in-house or domestically signals that ministries are open to (and politically rewarded for) domain-specific sovereign LLM deployments. Comparable use cases (customs, justice, social security, civil status) are logical follow-ons and match a self-hosted RAG/agent platform.
- Governance is split between at least three centres: Ministry of Higher Education (strategy author, Conseil scientifique, ENSIA), HCN (digital transformation, data centres, data governance) and the Ministry of Post and Telecommunications (Oran AI data centre). - Public AI compute is not yet available at scale (Oran expected 2026-2027), so near-term deployments must run on hardware the buyer or vendor procures; GPU import and FX constraints (section 7) therefore matter for delivery.
- Strategy documents are heavy on training targets and light on procurement mechanics; revenue from public AI will come project by project.

## 5. Identity documents and digitisation: biometric documents, e-government identity, ID verification demand

### Takeaway
Algeria has run biometric identity documents for a decade (passport, CNIBE since January 2016, points-based biometric driving licence since April 2018) and in February 2026 enacted a trust-services and electronic-identification law (Loi 26-02) that anchors digital identity on the CNIBE; but no usable issuance totals were retrieved, and the CNIBE chip is reportedly not yet exposed as an authentication service to banks or other private providers. In that interim, banks, Algérie Poste, PSPs and telecoms must verify identity from the physical documents, which is the demand window for ID OCR.

### Cited Findings
- The CNIBE (carte nationale d'identité biométrique électronique) was launched in January 2016 and the biometric points-based driving licence in April 2018; the Ministry of Interior presents the CNIBE as the key to creating citizens' digital identities and says its use for authentication is being generalised (ID4Africa 2026 presentation, May 2026) — [ID4Africa 2026, Algeria presentation (PDF)](https://id4africa.com/2026/files/T2-3-S3-4-Algeria-FINAL.pdf)
- The CNIBE is issued by the Ministry of Interior under Décret présidentiel 17-143, carries chips with fingerprint and facial data and an 18-digit national identification number (NIN) [LOW] — [algeriatech.news](https://algeriatech.news/?p=2263)
- Seven million biometric passports had been issued according to then-Interior Minister Noureddine Bedoui (undated article, pre-2019) [STALE] — [Algérie 360](https://www.algerie360.com/sept-millions-de-passeports-biometriques-delivres-a-ce-jour-ministre/)
- A 2019 World Bank ID4D listing gave 4.73 million for Algeria's biometric ID project [STALE: 2019] — [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/201909/id4d-digital-identity-projects)
- A single file now serves both CNIBE and biometric passport applications — [Algérie 360](https://www.algerie360.com/un-seul-et-meme-dossier-pour-lobtention-de-la-carte-didentite-et-du-passeport-biometriques-electroniques/)
- Since 24 June 2025 a criminal-record extract can be obtained on presentation of the CNIBE, biometric passport or biometric driving licence; a biometric document also suffices for a nationality certificate (March 2025) — [Observ'Algérie](https://observalgerie.com/2025/06/24/societe/les-demarches-pour-le-casier-judiciaire-simplifiee-en-algerie/); [AllAfrica/APS](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202503180487.html)
- Delivery delays: several weeks to months for biometric documents; six to eight weeks cited for passports — [Maghreb Emergent](https://maghrebemergent.news/fr/permis-et-passeports-biometriques-les-delais-de-delivrance-penalisent-les-citoyens/)

- Banks' KYC instruction (04-2026) requires collection of identity documents and verification of their consistency at onboarding and throughout the relationship, across channels — [Radio Algérie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/85371)
- Telecoms: operators must suspend any line whose holder is not identified, with a DZD 100,000 fine per unidentified SIM (undated article); ARPCE launched code *254# so subscribers can check SIMs registered in their name; ARPCE sanctioned all three operators financially — [Algérie 360](https://www.algerie360.com/letat-veut-responsabiliser-les-operateurs-de-telephonie-mobile-une-amende-de-100-000-da-pour-toute-puce-non-identifiee/); [Algérie 360](https://www.algerie360.com/les-algeriens-peuvent-desormais-traquer-les-cartes-sim-ouvertes-en-leur-nom-voici-comment/); [Radio Algérie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/54021)

- Nov 2025: Algeria-UK memorandum on electronic fingerprint analysis and biometric identification for the police — [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202511/algeria-to-get-uk-support-on-digital-forensics-biometrics-for-policing)

### Inferences
- Volume drivers for ID OCR are large even without issuance totals: about 13.7 million active dinar bank accounts, 21 million+ active Algérie Poste accounts and about 51.5 million mobile SIMs (2023) all carry periodic re-identification obligations (KYC refresh, SIM identification), and every new PSP wallet, DZ Mob Pay enrolment and online account opening needs document capture.
- The 2026 KYC instruction's "document consistency" check and the SIM-identification fines are concrete compliance hooks for automated document extraction and validation.
- Medium-term risk to an OCR-only product: once Loi 26-02's electronic-identification scheme and ANCE-licensed providers are operational, chip/NFC or API-based identity verification against the State registry could displace image OCR for Algerian nationals. OCR remains needed for legacy documents, foreign documents, and for back-office digitisation of archives.
- Trust-service providers must be licensed and host data in Algeria, which favours local integrators as ANCE-licensed relying-party or provider partners.

### Gaps
- No current issuance totals for CNIBE, biometric passports or biometric driving licences were retrieved; the ID4Africa 2026 PDF likely contains them but could not be opened. All numeric issuance figures found are pre-2020.
- No evidence was found on which eKYC/ID-verification vendors Algerian banks and telecoms currently use, or whether any bank does biometric (selfie/liveness) onboarding.
- Implementing decrees of Loi 26-02 (who may query the identity registry, on what terms) were not found.
- No ARPCE decision mandating electronic reading of the biometric card by operators, and nothing on eSIM identification, was found.

## 6. Digital forensics demand: buyers, cybercrime statistics, legal framework, procurement

### Takeaway
Recorded cybercrime is rising fast (DGSN: 13,204 cases and 7,815 suspects in 2025) and is handled by three institutional buyers of forensics capability: the DGSN (police, with its central cybercrime service and scientific-police labs), the Gendarmerie nationale (INCC at Bouchaoui and its cybercrime centre) and the justice system's specialised cybercrime jurisdiction, with the Defence-ministry ASSI framework and banks' security teams as secondary buyers. Procurement is opaque and security-classified: no public tender or vendor name for forensic tooling was found, and capability-building is channelled through state-to-state cooperation (UK, South Korea, EU/Council of Europe CyberSouth+).

### Inferences
- With roughly 13,000 police cases plus several thousand Gendarmerie cases a year and a certified-examiner pool reportedly in the dozens, there is a throughput bottleneck that case-management, triage and AI-assisted analysis software could address; the constraint is skilled examiners more than hardware.
- Buyers are few (two security forces, the justice ministry, ASSI/MDN-linked bodies), procurement is non-public and often bundled into foreign cooperation agreements. Sales will depend on clearances, pilots and institutional sponsorship rather than tenders; cycles will be long.
- A locally developed, locally supported platform with source-code access and no foreign telemetry fits the sovereignty posture of the 2025-2029 security strategy; it is also plausible that some foreign forensic suites face political or export-licensing obstacles in Algeria. This is an inference: no source was found on which foreign tools Algerian agencies use or avoid.
- Banks' security and fraud teams are a secondary, more accessible market for forensics/investigation tooling, given the AML and cyber obligations in sections 2 and 3; no evidence of actual bank purchases was found.
- A forensics platform would have to be validated against INCC's ISO/IEC 17025 accredited methods to produce court-admissible results; accreditation fit is a product requirement.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed Ordonnance 21-11 as the text creating the pôle pénal national (named in the brief); no case statistics for the pole were found.

- DGSN scientific-police laboratory structure for digital evidence (central and regional labs) was not retrieved.

## 7. Startup ecosystem and financing; constraints that favour local vendors

- Valuation benchmarks are scarce. - FX controls cut both ways: they shield local vendors from foreign SaaS competition (buyers cannot easily pay recurring foreign-currency subscriptions, and data-transfer rules compound this), but they also make it harder for the vendor to buy GPUs, foreign cloud and software licences.

- No Algerian AI or fintech startup funding round with amount or valuation was found for 2024-2026; names of notable Algerian AI/fintech startups and their rounds remain a gap.
- Yassir's later, larger round and current valuation were not retrieved.
- No text confirming a formal public-procurement preference margin for labelled startups; implementing texts for Loi 23-12 art. 41 were still pending in the sources found.
- No rule was found specifically on domiciliation, taxes or approvals for importing software/SaaS services by companies; the FX friction evidence is indirect (travel allowance, card limits) and partly from a commercial blog.
- Rules on importing IT hardware (GPUs/servers) were not researched.

### Inferences
- The AI-market figure (USD 0.5 bn in 2025) is larger than the same class of estimates for all IT consulting and for cybersecurity combined, which is implausible for realised enterprise AI software spend; it should be treated as a headline, not a base for sizing.
- A realistic serviceable market for the vendor's banking products is bounded by about 30 regulated institutions (plus new PSPs). At plausible enterprise licence values, fraud scoring plus KYC scoring in Algeria is a market of single-digit to low-double-digit millions of USD a year, not hundreds of millions. This is the writer's inference from buyer count; no published figure supports or contradicts it.
- The public-sector sovereign-AI and forensics segments are budget-driven and unsized; the best proxies are the count of public bodies now required to staff security units (Décret 26-07) and the "500+ digital projects for 2025-2026" pipeline cited by the HCN.
- Morocco is the natural benchmark for where Algerian payment usage and STR volumes could go: similar card base, roughly ten times the POS value, and more than six times the last-known Algerian STR count.
